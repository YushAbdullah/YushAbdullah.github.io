The real talk

For the rakyat, this budget is generous where it counts. A RM300 jump in the minimum wage and cash aid for millions put money in the hands of the people who spend it fastest.

But look at the scorecard. Salaries, pensions and debt interest alone eat most of operating spending. That leaves little room to manoeuvre, and the budget adds no new taxes to widen the revenue base.

“A baby step in deficit reduction.”

Lee Heng Guie, Socio-Economic Research Centre

So the gifts are real. The question is who pays for them later. Without broader revenue, the government is borrowing to cover the gap.

The case for slow

Cutting the deficit too fast has its own cost. Slash spending hard while the cost of living is already pening-level, and you hurt growth and the same households the budget is trying to help.

The deficit is going down, and revenue for 2026 came in above the original projection. Slow and steady is still progress.

Worth watching

The SME worry

Lee also flags a business-side worry. A RM2,000 wage floor and a RM2,500 graduate baseline could make SMEs think twice about hiring fresh staff.

If you're job hunting as a fresh grad, this is the one to keep an eye on.
